## Core stated principle

> "Different objects can produce the same numerical reading point without being identified with one another."

> "Their specifications remain distinct."

Demonstrated on the site via the reading point **5**: a hand's five digits, 𝔰𝔬(10)'s rank 5, five-dimensional space, a pentagon's five sides, and a music staff's five lines all share the numerical reading 5 without being the same object. Each supplies its own specification of that reading.

## The +5 constraint (mod-30 structure)

The site's arithmetic example: (1+1)×3×5=30. Every prime p>5 resists division by 2, 3, and 5, so its residue modulo 30 occupies one of eight classes: {1,7,11,13,17,19,23,29}. 30 positions reduce to 8 candidate reading points; occupying one of the eight is necessary for a prime greater than 5, but does not by itself establish primality.

The site states explicitly: "Specified readings remain distinct. Divisibility is the arithmetic reading. Physical quantities, including gravity, require their own physical specification. A shared reading point does not turn one specification into another." — this line is the source's own guard against exactly the kind of category error (treating a shared numerical value as a shared physical or structural claim) that this repository's verification pipeline exists to catch computationally.
