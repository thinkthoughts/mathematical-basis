## Redundant material once Theorem 3 precedes Theorem 1

Three specific spots, as requested:

1. **Item 4** (Section 1's "$d=m$ coincidence" remark). In discovery order this remark tells a
   reader who's only seen Theorem 1 "don't worry, a sharper condition exists elsewhere and it
   happens to coincide here." In result order, the reader has already seen Theorem 3, Theorem 2,
   and Theorem 1′ by the time they reach Theorem 1 — the general pattern ($d=\operatorname{rad}(m)$
   when $m\mid N$, further $d=m$ when $m$ is squarefree) has already been established twice over.
   This remark could likely shrink to one sentence, or be cut entirely as strictly implied by
   Theorem 2's own "$d=\operatorname{rad}(m)$" substitution plus squarefreeness of $m$. Marked
   MOVE + SHORTEN rather than DELETE CANDIDATE outright, since a one-sentence version may still be
   worth keeping as an explicit closing note — but the full paragraph as currently written is
   doing work that's already been done twice by the time a result-order reader reaches it.

2. **Item 7** (Section 2's post-proof remark pointing to "Section 3"). This is the clearest
   redundancy in the document. Once merged into the same subsection as item 18 (old §3.6, which
   makes the identical point in more detail — squarefreeness collapsing $T_{\min}$ into $P_k$
   exactly), item 7 adds nothing item 18 doesn't already say better. **Strong DELETE CANDIDATE.**

3. **Item 11** (Section 3's old opening paragraph). Not merely redundant — actively
   disorienting in result order, since it refers to Theorem 1 as already-known ("Theorem 1 was
   originally developed and verified only for...") before the reader has encountered it. This is
   the one genuinely **required rewrite** in the whole migration, not a deletion or a shortening.

## Open questions (not resolved here — flagging for decision before execution)

1. **How should Theorem 1 be presented once it's no longer the paper's first result?** Two
   defensible options, both compatible with "don't discard the discovery history, turn it into
   provenance":
   - **(a)** Keep Theorem 1's full original CRT proof (item 6) as the primary treatment, with old
     §3.6's specialization-derivation (item 18) folded in as a short closing remark ("this also
     follows directly by substitution into Theorem 3, confirming...") — preserves the standalone,
     self-contained character the original proof had in v3.1.
   - **(b)** Lead with the substitution-only derivation (matching how Theorem 2 and Theorem 1′ are
     both already handled), and demote the full CRT proof to a boxed/appendix-style aside labeled
     "original proof (v3.1), retained for comparison."
   This plan does not choose between them — it's a presentation decision, not a structural one,
   and affects how much text Theorem 1 occupies in the new Section 3.

2. **Where exactly does the hierarchy display (item 12) land — start of Section 2 or start of
   Section 3?** It's used to introduce the *specialization* walk (Theorem 3 → 2 → 1′ → 1), which
   argues for Section 3's opening. But it also names Theorem 3 before Theorem 3 has been stated
   in-line if placed there, which is fine (readers have already seen it in Section 2 by the time
   they reach Section 3) but worth confirming the intended reading order is "state Theorem 3 fully
   in Section 2, then Section 3 opens by re-displaying the hierarchy as a roadmap" rather than
   putting the roadmap before Theorem 3 has been proved.

3. **Does the Change Log need a companion for this pass?** Following the precedent set by
   `v3.2-pre-final-audit.md` (kept separate from the v3.1→v3.2 Change Log for the wording-only
   pass), this reordering pass would similarly warrant its own `v3.2-result-order-audit.md` once
   executed — recommended, not decided here, since it wasn't asked for in this planning step.
