# Mathematical Basis — Scope

## Purpose

This document specifies the scope of applying results from
mathematical-basis to other mathematical, scientific, and engineering
problems.

---

## General Distinction

Constraints can specify an admissible set.

This distinction can be useful independently of any particular theorem.

For example, if

\[
|x-A| \le \varepsilon,
\]

then the admissible readings are constrained to

\[
[A-\varepsilon,\;A+\varepsilon].
\]

This uses the general distinction between a reading and the set of
readings admitted by specified constraints.

---

## Direct Theorem Application

A direct application of a theorem requires its mathematical objects and
hypotheses to be specified and checked.

For the General Divisor Theorem, claims involving

\[
\gcd(a,d),
\qquad
T_{\min}=\operatorname{lcm}(m,\operatorname{rad}(N)),
\qquad
C=\frac{d}{\varphi(d)}
\]

require the corresponding divisor structure, including the specified
values of \(N,m,a,d\).

Without that structure, the general distinction may still apply, but
the theorem has not been applied.

---

## Scope Rule

\[
\boxed{
\text{general distinction}
\ne
\text{direct theorem application}
}
\]

The distinction may apply where the theorem does not.

A direct theorem application requires:

1. the theorem's mathematical objects to be specified;
2. a mapping from the problem to those objects;
3. the theorem's hypotheses to be checked;
4. conclusions to be limited to those supplied by the theorem.

---

## Reading Point

A reading point can specify a position within an admissible space
without specifying every interpretation of that position.

Accordingly,

\[
\boxed{
\text{specified reading point}
\ne
\text{specified cause}
}
\]

where causal interpretation lies outside the theorem unless an
additional mathematical argument establishes it.
