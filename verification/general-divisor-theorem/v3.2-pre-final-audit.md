## Edit 2 — Minimality conclusion in Theorem 3's proof

**Before:** "...$mR\mid t$. So every period is at least $mR$, and $mR$ is itself a period:
$T_{\min}=mR$ is exactly minimal."

**After:** "...$mR\mid t$. Thus $mR\mid t$ for every positive period $t$. Since $mR$ itself is a
period, it is the exact minimal positive period: $T_{\min}=mR$."

This states the divisibility fact the proof actually establishes ($mR\mid t$), rather than the
weaker inequality ($t\ge mR$) that the divisibility fact implies but is not itself. No step of the
proof preceding this sentence was touched; the logical content of the conclusion is unchanged
(divisibility still implies the inequality, so nothing that followed from the old sentence stops
following from the new one) — only the precision of what's asserted changed.

## Edit 3 — $N=5,m=6,a=2$ example characterization

**Before:** "...confirming that $\gcd(a,d)=1$ is a genuinely sharper admissibility condition than
$\gcd(a,m)=1$, not merely a cosmetic restatement of it."

**After:** "...confirming that $\gcd(a,d)=1$ is the exact admissibility condition and is strictly
weaker than $\gcd(a,m)=1$ in general — it admits strictly more values of $a$, since $d$ contains
only the primes shared between $m$ and $N$, not merely a cosmetic restatement of the older
condition."

The numerical content of the example (the computation of $d$, $R$, $T_{\min}$, the enumerated set
$\{2,8,14,20,26\}$, the count $4=\varphi(5)$, and $C(5,6)=1$) is untouched — only the closing
characterization changed, from "sharper" (ambiguous between stricter and weaker) to the precise
"exact... and strictly weaker," matching what the example actually demonstrates: $\gcd(a,d)=1$
accepts an $a$ that $\gcd(a,m)=1$ would reject.
